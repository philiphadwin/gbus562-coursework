Purchasing Approval Matrix

Current version. Owner: Finance Department, Procurement.

When a purchase order is required

Any purchase of goods or services over $1,000 needs an approved purchase order before you commit to the vendor. Purchases of $1,000 or less may be made on a corporate card. Splitting one purchase into several smaller orders to stay under an approval level is a policy violation.

Approval levels by purchase amount

The total amount of the purchase decides who must approve the purchase order:

  • Up to $2,500: your manager.
  • More than $2,500 and up to $10,000: your department director.
  • More than $10,000 and up to $50,000: your department director and the Vice President of Finance.
  • More than $50,000: your department director, the Vice President of Finance, and the Chief Financial Officer.

For a contract that runs longer than one year, the amount is the total value over the whole contract, not the yearly cost.

Competitive quotes

Purchases over $25,000 need at least three written quotes from different vendors, attached to the purchase order. If only one vendor can supply what is needed, attach a sole-source justification signed by your department director instead.

Purchases from new vendors

A vendor that Copperline has not paid before must be set up in the vendor master record by Accounts Payable before a purchase order can be issued. Setup takes about five business days. See the Vendor Payment Terms document for the steps Accounts Payable follows.

Software, subscriptions, and cloud services

Every purchase of software, a software subscription, or a cloud service needs an IT Security review in addition to the approvals for its amount. This applies at any amount, including free trials that will collect company data. Submit the IT Security review request before the purchase order. Purchasing will not issue the order until IT Security has signed off.

Capital equipment

Machinery, vehicles, and other equipment with a useful life over three years and a cost over $5,000 are capital purchases. Capital purchases must also be in the approved annual capital budget. A capital purchase that is not in the budget needs the Chief Financial Officer's approval at any amount.
