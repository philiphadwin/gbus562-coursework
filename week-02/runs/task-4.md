### System

You work for Wildframe Coffee Roasters, a specialty coffee roaster that sells beans online and through a monthly subscription. Our customers are environmentally conscious urban professionals. Our brand voice is warm, plainspoken, and a little irreverent, with no corporate jargon.

### You

As our operations lead, help us decide where to run one four-week pop-up stand this spring. Our total rent budget is $5,000 for the four weeks.

Here is weekly data from comparable past events:
- Riverside Farmers Market: 9,000 visitors per week; 2% of visitors buy; average sale $14 (mostly whole-bean bags); rent $900 per week.
- Union Station concourse: 30,000 visitors per week; 0.5% of visitors buy; average sale $6 (mostly single cups); rent $2,000 per week.
- Campus library atrium: 6,000 visitors per week; 3% of visitors buy; average sale $5 (single cups); rent $600 per week.

Think through this step by step before deciding:
1. Check each location's four-week rent against the $5,000 budget.
2. Calculate weekly buyers, weekly revenue, and weekly revenue minus rent for each location.
3. Total those figures over four weeks.
4. Consider anything beyond the numbers that matters for a coffee roaster that sells beans and subscriptions.
5. Choose one location.

Format requirements:

# Step 2 & 3: Weekly and Four-Week Performance

**Riverside Farmers Market**
- Buyers/week: 9,000 × 2% = 180
- Revenue/week: 180 × $14 = $2,520
- Revenue minus rent/week: $2,520 − $900 = $1,620
- **4-week totals: 720 buyers, $10,080 revenue, $3,600 rent, $6,480 net**

**Union Station Concourse**
- Buyers/week: 30,000 × 0.5% = 150
- Revenue/week: 150 × $6 = $900
- Revenue minus rent/week: $900 − $2,000 = **−$1,100** (loses money every week)
- **4-week totals: 600 buyers, $3,600 revenue, $8,000 rent, −$4,400 net**

**Campus Library Atrium**
- Buyers/week: 6,000 × 3% = 180
- Revenue/week: 180 × $5 = $900
- Revenue minus rent/week: $900 − $600 = $300
- **4-week totals: 720 buyers, $3,600 revenue, $2,400 rent, $1,200 net**

# Step 4: Beyond the Spreadsheet

- **Union Station is a non-starter** — it blows the budget and loses money. High foot traffic doesn't matter if almost nobody's stopping to buy, and when they do, it's a quick single cup, not a real coffee purchase.
- **Riverside's customers are buying whole-bean bags**, not single cups. That's the exact behavior of someone who could become a subscriber — they're already brewing at home and care enough to seek out good beans. Farmers market shoppers also skew toward the "environmentally conscious urban professional" crowd we're built for. This is as much a subscription-acquisition event as a sales event.
- **Campus library buyers are grabbing a quick cup between classes** — convenient, but it's a one-off transaction with little connection to our actual product (bags) or our subscription model.
- Riverside also leaves $1,400 of budget unused even after rent — room for samples, subscription sign-up cards, or a punch-card deal to turn market regulars into subscribers.

# Step 5: Decision

Riverside wins on every count that matters: it's within budget, it generates more than 5x the net revenue of the next-best option, and it puts us in front of exactly the people who'd want a Wildframe subscription. This isn't a close call.

Recommendation: Riverside Farmers Market
